\textit{Temporal generalization}. To verify that performance does not depend on a particular split within 2019, the framework was trained on the preceding winters and applied to an entire unseen winter (2018, 2019; Table~\ref{tab_crosswinter}). At $h=1$, the best model outperformed the naive forecast in 6 of 8 station--winter combinations, with relative MAE between 0.81 and 1.09. \textit{Spatial generalization}. When the stochastic component was trained at one station and applied to another (Table~\ref{tab_crossstation}, $h=1$), the MAE averaged 1.05 times that of the local model (range 0.80--1.45); the transferred model outperformed the target station's naive forecast in 12 of 36 cases. The model trained on the four stations pooled achieved a mean relative MAE of 0.94 with respect to the local model. This indicates the extent to which the short-term residual dynamics are shared across the network and are therefore transferable to stations with insufficient history.
